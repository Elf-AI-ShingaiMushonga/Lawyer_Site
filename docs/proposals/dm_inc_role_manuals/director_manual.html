Director Role

Use the ERP as a leadership operating console, not just a matter browser.

A director should be able to see where the firm is exposed, where work is bottlenecked, where revenue is leaking, which matters need intervention, and which people need leadership attention. The platform already contains the signals for that if it is used consistently.

Leadership role Admin-family authority Analytics access Personnel management
Default mode

Extra control

The dedicated Director Personnel screen lets directors assign attorneys to their team and monitor workload, overdue tasks, and utilization.

What the director role should own

  • Work allocation and team pressure: monitor the task queue, overdue load, and attorney utilization.
  • Matter governance: identify files that need partner attention, scope reset, risk escalation, or staffing change.
  • Revenue discipline: keep invoices, AR, time review, expenses, and trust controls inside a repeatable operating cadence.
  • Platform governance: keep admin settings, portal users, automation rules, and audit visibility under control.

What the director should avoid

  • Doing everything personally: broad system authority is for control and intervention, not for bypassing operating ownership.
  • Using analytics as an afterthought: the director role is most valuable when it actively manages signals, not just final outcomes.
  • Treating admin power casually: user, portal, and rules changes can have serious legal and financial consequences.
  • Ignoring workflow hygiene: poor team task discipline makes the leadership surfaces far less reliable.

Operating Rhythm

A practical cadence for how a director should move through the system.

Directors get the most value when they use the ERP rhythmically. A consistent review cadence turns the platform into an early-warning system rather than a passive record.

Daily cadence

  1. 1
    Open the dashboard in Team Command.
    Review Priority Inbox, legal desk pressure, and at-risk matters before anything else.
  2. 2
    Open the matter or person behind the signal.
    Use matter summary, workspace, or Director Personnel rather than making decisions from the dashboard alone.
  3. 3
    Decide whether to coach, reassign, escalate, or approve.
    Do not let signals sit unresolved simply because they are visible.
  4. 4
    Switch to Revenue & Risk when needed.
    Check time review, invoices, AR, and trust where the day’s issues are financial rather than delivery-led.

Weekly and monthly cadence

  1. 1
    Weekly: review workload, burnout, team utilization, overdue tasks, and matter risk posture.
  2. 2
    Weekly: inspect client-response and billing-leakage queues to prevent avoidable service and revenue drift.
  3. 3
    Month-end: move into Revenue & Risk mode and use AR Aging, trial balance, auditor reports, trust reports, and invoice review surfaces.
  4. 4
    Quarterly: review admin configuration, portal access posture, template drift, and audit patterns.
A strong director rhythm starts broad and then goes narrow: dashboard signal, focused screen, intervention decision, follow-through. The ERP is most valuable when it shortens the distance between “we have a problem” and “someone has acted on it”.
Oversight Playbook

Where directors should intervene and what each intervention should look like.

Directors should not use the ERP merely to observe. They should use it to redirect work, authorize movement, and clean up weak operating patterns before they become client, fee, or governance failures.

Leadership concern Best screen What the director should do
At-risk matter Matter Summary and Matter Workspace Review executive summary, timeline, tasks, next-best moves, and live context. Decide whether staffing, scope, or client communication needs intervention.
Team overload or weak utilization Analytics Workload and Director Personnel Check who is overloaded, underused, or accumulating overdue work. Rebalance assignments and review whether task ownership is realistic.
Revenue lag or debtor exposure Invoices, AR Aging, Time Review, Profitability Identify whether the problem is capture, approval, billing delay, collections pressure, or low realization on a specific matter or client.
Client communication failure Priority Inbox and Client Message Center Open the delayed thread, confirm who owns the response, and ensure the firm answers in-thread with clear matter context.
Portal or platform governance issue Portal User Administration, Admin Command Center, Audit Log Review whether the problem is access, visibility, user setup, control drift, or rules configuration before changing production settings.

When to step into matter detail personally

  • Risk is high or critical and the executive summary is stale or weak.
  • Deadlines or milestones look exposed and task movement does not match urgency.
  • Client pressure is rising and the response trail suggests drift or fragmentation.
  • Billing posture and matter reality conflict, for example where work is happening but revenue is not moving.

When to stay at portfolio level

  • The issue is trend-led, not file-led, such as rising team overload or falling realization.
  • The question is staffing or governance, not legal substance.
  • Finance or admin owners can resolve it once the signal is clearly assigned.
  • The right intervention is policy or cadence rather than hand-editing one record.
Guardrails

Broad access is useful only when it stays disciplined.

Directors can see and change a great deal in the current implementation. That makes the role powerful, but it also means poor habits at director level can create confusion, rework, and governance risk very quickly.

Director guardrails

  • Keep ownership clear. Escalate and assign; do not silently absorb work that should remain with the operating owner.
  • Use admin power sparingly. Change users, rules, portal visibility, and templates only with a clear governance reason.
  • Use analytics to coach, not punish. Signals should trigger support, reallocation, or process correction before blame.
  • Preserve thread discipline with clients. Use the Client Message Center to answer in context rather than creating scattered conversations.

Director go-live checklist

  • Dashboard reviewed daily by at least one director or delegated leadership owner.
  • Director team assignments set so personnel analytics and team scope are meaningful.
  • Revenue and trust review cadence agreed with the finance / cost admin role.
  • Portal governance owner named for client identities, visibility, and escalation.
  • Admin changes documented so the platform does not drift without a decision trail.
Current build note: directors fall into the admin-family access group. That is operationally useful, but DM-Inc should treat director access as privileged governance access and keep named users limited and well trained.
