Daily Work Pattern

A strong junior-attorney rhythm starts with the queue and ends with clean records.

The platform gives junior attorneys a practical sequence: dashboard signal, matter workspace, task flow, drafting, time capture, and client follow-through. The order matters because it keeps execution aligned with supervision.

Recommended daily sequence

  1. 1
    Start in the dashboard.
    Check assigned work, priority inbox items that touch your matters, and today’s calendar pressure.
  2. 2
    Open the relevant matter workspace.
    Use the matter as the execution context before touching documents or timekeeping.
  3. 3
    Move the tasks, not just the work.
    Task status, due dates, ownership, and next steps should reflect reality as the day evolves.
  4. 4
    Capture time close to the work.
    Use timers or manual entries before the narrative quality deteriorates.
  5. 5
    Leave the file clearer than you found it.
    Update timeline, notes, summary inputs, or client thread context where the matter has actually moved.

Main screens to keep open mentally

  • Dashboard: assigned work queue, priority inbox, legal desk.
  • Matter Workspace: the current state of the file and the fastest path into next actions.
  • Matter Tasks: where effort becomes coordinated and reviewable.
  • Document Workbench and Matter DMS: where drafting and final document handling should live.
  • Time Capture Desk: where the day’s billable story becomes commercially useful.
Matter Execution

What good junior-attorney execution looks like inside the ERP.

Junior attorneys often sit closest to the live work. That makes this role critical to data quality, task integrity, document discipline, and keeping the matter understandable to the rest of the team.

Area of work Best screen Expected behaviour
Task execution Matter Tasks and Workspace Update status, use due dates properly, and keep task ownership visible rather than managing everything through private reminders.
Drafting Matter Document Workbench Create, revise, comment, and collaborate inside the matter before publishing stable versions into DMS.
File context Matter Summary, timeline, activity feed Refresh key dates, status movement, and matter notes so the file reflects the current position.
Research and precedent retrieval Search Command Center and Knowledge Base Use search to find matters, tasks, reference material, contacts, and DMS content instead of rebuilding context manually.
Document filing and evidence Matter DMS, email capture, versions Ensure important documents reach the matter repository with the right metadata and visible version chain.

Habits that help the supervising attorney

  • Keep summaries current enough to brief upward quickly.
  • Use timelines and notes to show what changed, not just that work was done.
  • Keep draft chains clean so review is faster and safer.
  • Link work back to matter context before handing it off.

Warning signs you are working outside the system

  • The task board does not match the real workload.
  • Documents are circulating by filename only.
  • Time entries are recreated from memory at the end of the week.
  • Leadership must ask verbally for file posture that should already be visible.
Collaboration and Commercials

Execution is stronger when legal work, client communication, and fee capture stay connected.

Junior attorneys often sit at the point where the legal task, the client expectation, and the billable narrative meet. The system is designed to keep those three things in one loop.

Client and team collaboration

  • Use the Client Message Center for portal replies. Reply inside the existing thread wherever possible.
  • Use the AI-assisted client update tools carefully. They can speed drafting but must still be reviewed as legal communications.
  • Use notes and activity for internal context. Important work should not vanish into side channels.
  • Use contacts and search to avoid context loss. The platform is designed to reduce duplicate reconstruction of known information.

Commercial responsibilities

  • Time capture is part of the legal workflow. Use timers or manual entries before the story of the work fades.
  • Keep narratives bill-ready. Clean time entries reduce review friction and billing leakage later.
  • Submit expenses where relevant. Disbursements should enter the matter’s commercial record promptly.
  • Escalate commercial pressure early. If the matter is moving but time, expenses, or billing posture are not, raise it before it becomes month-end rework.
Current build note: junior attorneys have broad legal-role permissions, including billing-related surfaces. DM-Inc should still train junior attorneys to use commercial actions within the firm’s supervisory and approval structure rather than assuming the system itself will limit every edge case.
Checklist

What the role should be able to do confidently after training.

If a junior attorney can perform the items below consistently, the platform is being used in the way the build intends.

Junior attorney success checklist

  • Can move from dashboard to matter workspace without losing context.
  • Can update tasks, deadlines, and summary signals as work happens.
  • Can draft, comment, and publish documents using workbench and DMS properly.
  • Can capture time cleanly and promptly.
  • Can reply to client portal threads in the right matter context.

Escalate when

  • The matter scope, risk, or client expectation changes materially.
  • The work cannot be completed inside the current deadline or staffing pattern.
  • A finance or trust implication appears outside normal matter handling.
  • You need admin, portal-governance, or rule changes rather than matter-level action.
  • You cannot explain the current file posture from the ERP alone.
