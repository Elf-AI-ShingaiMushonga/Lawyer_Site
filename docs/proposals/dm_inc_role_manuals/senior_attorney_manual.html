Senior Attorney Role

Lead the file from instruction to outcome, with legal quality and commercial discipline intact.

The senior attorney is not only doing legal work. This role is responsible for keeping the matter legible, actionable, on time, client-ready, and billable. The ERP should become the operating layer for that discipline rather than a place where records are updated after the fact.

What this role should own in practice

  • Matter structure: summary quality, team setup, task hygiene, timeline clarity, and document discipline.
  • Delivery rhythm: work should move through workspace, tasks, deadlines, and drafting in a way the team can actually follow.
  • Commercial hygiene: time should be captured, reviewed, and converted into billing outcomes before value leaks.
  • Client readiness: updates, portal replies, and matter status should be coherent and defensible at any point.

What this role should not outsource to memory

  • Deadlines and milestones: they belong in the matter timeline and calendar views.
  • Next steps: they belong in tasks, not in private inboxes or ad hoc notes.
  • Draft version history: it belongs in the workbench and DMS chain, not in scattered filenames.
  • Client-response ownership: it should be visible in the portal message center or the matter operating context.
Matter Lifecycle

The senior attorney’s end-to-end matter workflow inside the platform.

The goal is not simply to open a matter and then work around the system. The goal is to set the matter up so the rest of the team can execute inside a reliable, reviewable operating structure.

  1. 1
    Open or convert the matter correctly.
    Use intake or matter creation to establish core client, practice area, risk, and staffing context as early as possible.
  2. 2
    Shape the matter summary.
    Complete the executive summary, archetype fields, legal category, risk status, and timeline so the matter can be understood quickly by leadership and support users.
  3. 3
    Build the execution loop.
    Use tasks, workspace, deadlines, and matter activity so the file shows what has happened, what must happen next, and who owns each move.
  4. 4
    Control drafting and evidence.
    Draft inside the matter workbench where collaboration, comments, and DMS publication can be managed cleanly.
  5. 5
    Keep commercial signals current.
    Time, expenses, invoice generation, and AR attention should run in parallel with legal work, not after the matter narrative is already stale.

Delivery Workflows

The working habits that keep legal execution visible, reusable, and billable.

Senior attorneys should model the platform behaviours they want from their teams. If the lead attorney keeps the matter structured, the rest of the team usually follows that pattern.

Daily delivery pattern

  • Start in the dashboard or current workspace. Use current matter context, not memory, to decide what moves first.
  • Open the task radar early. Ensure overdue work, imminent deadlines, and delegated actions are explicit.
  • Draft in the workbench. Use comments, collaborator presence, and publication into DMS instead of fragmented document handling.
  • Update the matter trail as work happens. Keep timeline, activity, next-best moves, and summary signals current enough for another attorney to take over if needed.

Client and intake pattern

  • Use CRM for intake and conflict checks. Keep lead progression, follow-ups, quote posture, and conflict outcomes in one place.
  • Use the Client Message Center for portal replies. Stay inside the relevant thread and link responses back to the correct matter context.
  • Use AI-generated summaries carefully. Draft client updates and executive summaries from the system, but review them as legal work product.
  • Use secure links and uploads intentionally. Share only what is needed and keep client collaboration aligned with the matter’s visibility posture.

Commercial Control

Legal delivery is only complete when time, billing, and matter economics keep pace with the work.

Senior attorneys do not need to become finance administrators, but they do need to prevent avoidable commercial drift. The platform gives them enough visibility and direct capability to do that.

Commercial actions a senior attorney should use

  • Time Capture Desk: capture work early and keep narrative quality high.
  • Time Review: clean up or approve time before billing windows are missed.
  • Invoices and invoice detail: review matter billing output, status, and adjustments when the file needs fee movement.
  • Expenses: submit and approve disbursement items that belong in the matter’s financial story.

What to watch commercially

  • Billing leakage: unbilled approved time or stale entries visible in the dashboard.
  • Realization risk: heavy work on the file with weak billing or collections conversion.
  • Client expectation gaps: a matter that is moving legally but not visibly from the client’s point of view.
  • Matter economics: budget watch status, high effort on low-value tasks, or expense patterns not being surfaced.
Current implementation note: senior attorneys have broad billing and analytics access in the platform. DM-Inc should still define an internal operating rule for which approval-heavy or adjustment-heavy actions are expected to be handled by finance versus legal leadership.
Checklist

What “good use” of the platform looks like for a senior attorney.

The checklist below is a practical benchmark for live operation, not a theoretical wishlist.

Senior attorney operating checklist

  • Every live matter has a usable summary.
  • Deadlines, tasks, and next moves are visible.
  • Drafting stays inside workbench and DMS discipline.
  • Client replies happen in context, not in scattered threads.
  • Time and expenses are not trailing the legal work materially.

Escalate when

  • The matter’s risk posture changes materially.
  • The team cannot deliver inside the current staffing model.
  • Client pressure or debtor exposure requires director or finance attention.
  • The file’s quality can no longer be protected through normal team supervision.
  • Admin or rules changes are needed rather than matter-level action.
