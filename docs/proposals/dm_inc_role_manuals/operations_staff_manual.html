Operations Staff Manual

This guide is for operations staff who support the platform operationally through internal IT service workflows, queue handling, and asset control rather than substantive legal casework.

Role posture

Operations staff is a support-role family with dedicated access to the helpdesk and asset registry. The role also has read-only or limited visibility in some business areas but should not be trained as a casework-authority role.

Helpdesk Workflow

Run the helpdesk as a visible queue, not a collection of informal requests.

The helpdesk module is designed for internal service management. Good use means requests arrive with enough detail, are assigned visibly, carry status honestly, and keep resolution notes in the record.

Core helpdesk loop

  1. 1
    Capture the issue properly.
    Subject, category, priority, description, and linked asset should be completed as cleanly as possible.
  2. 2
    Triage and assign visibly.
    Status and ownership should reflect reality so the queue can be managed operationally.
  3. 3
    Keep ticket history inside the record.
    Use comments, updates, and resolution summaries rather than private chat as the real service history.
  4. 4
    Close only with a usable resolution note.
    Tickets should help future support decisions, not merely disappear.

What operations staff should monitor

  • Open vs resolved load so the queue stays believable.
  • Critical or high-priority tickets that affect user productivity or client delivery.
  • Unassigned tickets that indicate queue neglect or ownership ambiguity.
  • Repeated incidents against the same asset that suggest replacement or root-cause action.

Asset Workflow

The asset registry should explain what exists, who holds it, and what is going wrong with it.

The asset module is not just a list of devices. It is the operational memory for assignment, status, warranty, and ticket linkage.

What belongs in the asset record

  • Asset tag and asset name kept unique and searchable.
  • Type and status such as laptop, phone, repair, assigned, retired.
  • Assigned user where the asset is in service.
  • Vendor, serial, location, purchase date, and warranty where available.
  • Notes and open-ticket context where they materially improve support quality.

Useful operating habits

  • Update records immediately after assignment or status change.
  • Use consistent asset tags.
  • Review repair and warranty posture regularly.
  • Link tickets to assets whenever practical.
  • Use the register to support procurement and replacement decisions.
Operations staff do not need wide legal-system authority to add real value. Clean IT records, clear ticket history, and good service assignment materially improve the reliability of the entire platform.
Boundaries

This role supports legal operations; it does not become legal operations.

The role model is explicit that operations staff should not be trained as substantive casework editors. Clarity here protects both delivery and governance.

What this role is not for

  • No substantive casework ownership.
  • No primary billing or trust control role.
  • No analytics leadership role.
  • No direct admin-template or firm-rule ownership.

When to escalate

  • Security or access issues that require admin-family action.
  • Repeated operational incidents that suggest policy, procurement, or architecture problems.
  • Business-process issues that are being reported through IT symptoms.
  • Any request that crosses into legal judgment or finance control.
Operations staff do have privileged operational access, especially around internal IT workflows. That is precisely why MFA discipline, audit hygiene, and clear ticket ownership matter for this role.
Checklist

Operations staff are ready when support work becomes measurable and controlled.

Ready-for-use checklist

  • Can triage, assign, update, and close tickets properly.
  • Can maintain the asset registry without duplicate or ambiguous records.
  • Can link assets and tickets where it improves support quality.
  • Can distinguish platform support from business-owner decisions.
  • Can keep operational changes inside the system record.

Service quality signs

  • Unassigned tickets do not linger.
  • Resolution notes are strong enough for future reuse.
  • Repair and warranty signals are visible before users escalate urgently.
  • Internal users know where to submit issues rather than using side channels.
  • Operational data is clean enough for leadership review.
