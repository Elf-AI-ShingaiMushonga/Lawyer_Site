Slide 02

Sign in, orient yourself, and learn the navigation pattern.

The top navigation is the lawyer’s control rail. The first five minutes of adoption should focus on how to enter the site, where the primary modules live, and how to move without losing context.

Sign-In Flow

What to tell every lawyer

  1. Open the site root and click Open Lawyer Site.
  2. Enter your firm email and your normal password.
  3. After sign-in, you land on the internal dashboard.
  4. If you were sent a direct link, the site will still bring you back to the requested page after login.

Navigation Pattern

What lives in the top shell

  • Dashboard for the day’s workload and priority inbox.
  • Matters for searching, filtering, and opening client work.
  • Calendar for due dates and docketing visibility.
  • Billing for invoice and financial visibility.
  • Jump / command menu for fast switching to modules like helpdesk, knowledge base, portal, or analytics.

What to train

Matter search discipline

  • Search by matter number when available.
  • Use client name or matter title when a number is not known.
  • Use directory filters instead of opening multiple tabs and guessing.
  • Once inside the matter, keep work anchored there so notes, documents, tasks, and deadlines stay tied to the right client record.

Inside the matter

Documents and DMS

Upload, review, and work with matter documents inside the matter context so the record stays complete and searchable.

Training line:

If the document belongs to a matter, put it in that matter rather than keeping it in local folders or email only.

Inside the matter

Tasks and responsibility

Use tasks to allocate work, track completion, and make due dates explicit instead of informal.

Training line:

If work must happen later or by someone else, it should become a task.

Inside the matter

Notes and activity trail

Capture substance, decisions, and follow-ups inside the record so another lawyer can reconstruct the file quickly.

Training line:

If it changes legal judgment, instruction, or risk, it belongs in the matter trail.

Inside the matter

Calendar and deadlines

Use the calendar surfaces for date-sensitive work so the system, not memory, carries the deadline burden.

Training line:

If a date matters, capture it where the team can see it.

Slide 05

Lawyer work is more than matter review: it is execution, follow-up, and client movement.

Once the right matter is open, the lawyer should use the site to move the file forward, not merely read it. The core disciplines are deadlines, follow-up, document flow, and client communication.

Matter execution

Use the workspace to move the file

  • Review what is due, what is blocked, and who owns the next action.
  • Update or create tasks when responsibilities change.
  • Attach or version the documents that support the next legal step.
  • Record client instructions, hearing preparation, research outcomes, and negotiation points directly into the matter record.

Client movement

Use portal and knowledge surfaces deliberately

  • Use the client portal when the client should see uploads, invoices, or message threads in a structured channel.
  • Use the knowledge base when you need internal precedent, policy, or process guidance.
  • Use the command menu to jump rather than backtracking through the full navigation.
A

Review the file

Confirm status, deadlines, latest activity, and the client’s latest instruction.

B

Advance the work

Draft, upload, assign, or respond. The goal is file movement, not passive reading.

C

Set the next trigger

Create the next task, deadline, or client follow-up so the matter does not stall.

D

Capture the trail

Leave enough evidence in the matter record that another lawyer can understand what changed.

The simplest training rule

If a lawyer leaves a matter without recording the outcome, the work may still be legally correct but operationally invisible. The site exists to prevent invisible work.

Slide 06

Time capture is part of the legal workflow, not an afterthought.

Lawyers should understand the time desk as a quality-control screen between work done and revenue realized. If time is captured late or poorly, billing and audit quality degrade immediately.

Training emphasis

When to capture time

  • Immediately after finishing a meaningful block of work.
  • At the moment you switch matters.
  • Before the end of the day, not at the end of the week.

Quality emphasis

What good entries sound like

  • Specific and professional.
  • Connected to the legal purpose of the work.
  • Free from vague language such as "miscellaneous admin."

Tools available

Timers and AI assist

The site includes timer management and a narrative drafting assist on the time desk. These help lawyers capture work closer to real time and clean up narrative quality.

Downstream impact

Why lawyers should care

Clear time entries support invoice generation, approvals, client confidence, and auditability. Bad time hurts both revenue and trust.

Slide 07

Use the surrounding modules when the matter requires client exchange, internal knowledge, or IT support.

The lawyer site is broader than matter management. Lawyers should know when to step into portal, knowledge, billing visibility, or helpdesk rather than trying to solve those needs through email alone.

Client-facing

Client Portal

Use the portal for structured client communication, document exchange, uploads, and invoice visibility where the client should interact with the firm digitally.

Route awareness:

/portal/login is the portal entry point, but lawyers usually launch portal work from the internal site.

Internal knowledge

Knowledge Base

Use the knowledge base to find internal articles, standard guidance, or firm process references instead of asking the same questions repeatedly.

Practical use:

New lawyers can use this for process memory while senior lawyers use it for consistency.

Financial awareness

Billing and trust visibility

Lawyers may not own every financial action, but they should understand the billing and trust state of their matters because it affects client conversations and urgency.

Practical use:

Check invoice progress and financial status before a client billing discussion.

Internal support

Helpdesk and linked assets

Lawyers can submit internal IT tickets through the helpdesk. If the issue relates to a specific laptop, dock, monitor, or device, they can link the ticket to the asset so operations can triage faster.

Route awareness:

/ops/helpdesk is for lawyers; the asset registry itself is primarily for support and admin roles.

Slide 08

The best training outcome is a repeatable daily rhythm.

Adoption succeeds when lawyers can explain their own rhythm inside the platform. This last slide is the simplest behavior model to reinforce in rollout sessions.

08:00
Start on the dashboard.

Review the priority inbox, check personal tasks, and choose the work that changes risk or client outcome first.

08:10
Open the matter and move it forward.

Use the matter directory to enter the right file. Update documents, tasks, notes, dates, and follow-ups in context.

During the day
Capture time as the work happens.

Use timers or manual entries. Keep narratives clean enough that billing and client review will make sense later.

Before close of day
Clear loose ends.

Set the next task or deadline, check portal or billing implications, and open a helpdesk ticket if an IT problem blocked the work.

Three rules for adoption

  • If the work matters, it belongs in the matter record.
  • If the time was spent, it belongs in the time desk.
  • If the platform or device failed, it belongs in helpdesk.

Presenter close

The goal is not for lawyers to memorize every screen. The goal is for them to trust the system as the place where legal work, operational follow-through, and billing readiness stay connected.
